JUNIOR ASTRONAUT: Lunar Survival Mission  
=====================================================================

HOW TO PLAY (Windows 10 / 11)
  1. Extract the junior astronaut archive file. 
  2. Open the release file from the junior astronaut archive file.
  3. Double click the "Play Junior Astronaut".
  4. The game opens in your web browser. Keep the small black window open
     while you play; close it when you are done.

  No internet and no installs are needed. Use Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge
  for the best result. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click
  "More info" -> "Run anyway" (the launcher only starts a local web server
  for the game on this computer).

  Don't open game\index.html directly: browsers block a 3D game that is
  opened straight from a file, which is why the launcher is there.

CONTROLS
  Mouse - look around (click the game to capture the mouse, Esc frees it)
  W A S D - walk     Shift - run     Space - jump
  E - use / scan (hold E for repairs)     F - helmet lamp
  K - Knowledge Center     C - crew panel     T - settlement panel (Mission 9)     Esc - pause
  Enter - close knowledge cards

  From Mission 2 you can walk into the habitat (HAB-1) through the airlock:
  inside, your suit oxygen refills and there are stations to use.
  Space skips the airlock walk.

  Settings -> Graphics: choose Low / Medium / High for the computer's speed.
  Settings -> Language: English or Burmese.

ON A MAC OR LINUX
  Open a terminal in the "game" folder and run:  python3 -m http.server 8765
  then open http://localhost:8765 in the browser.

SECRET DISCOVERIES AND EASTER EGGS (WHERE TO FIND THEM)
=====================================================================
Each mission hides secrets on the Moon. The distance and direction are
measured from the place named at the start of each mission. Walk (or drive
the rover) there and press E.

Mission 1 - from the lander
  - Orange glass beads: 37 m west. An orange patch of glittering beads on
    the ground.
  - A brand-new crater: 39 m south-east. A small bright crater with white
    rays around it.
  - The Moon golf shot (easter egg): 33 m north-east. A golf ball and club
    lie on the ground. You swing, and the camera follows the ball.

Mission 2 - from the base
  - A rock from somewhere else: 64 m north-west. A dark iron meteorite in a
    small dark pit.
  - The rock the Sun broke: 73 m north-east. A boulder split into three
    pieces.

Mission 3
  - Peaks of almost eternal light: about 136 m south-south-east, on the
    highest ridge near the base. A survey pole with a sun sensor on top.
  - Earthshine: 64 m west. A camera on a tripod pointed at Earth.
  - Hammer and feather (easter egg): 66 m north-north-east. A hammer and a
    feather on a flat rock. You drop them, and in slow motion they land
    together.

Mission 4
  - The shrinking Moon: 127 m south-west. A long low cliff with an orange
    flag at its foot. Press E at the flag.
  - Moonquakes: 75 m east. An old gold-foil seismometer with solar panels.
  - A photo left on the Moon (easter egg): 117 m south, just behind the
    lander. A small photo lying on the ground.

Mission 5
  - Rock full of bubbles: 98 m east. A dark boulder covered in round holes.
  - Moon soil remembers the Sun: 103 m north-west. A metal soil-sample tube
    stuck in the ground.
  - The zero-g indicator (easter egg): 43 m north. A teddy bear in a space
    suit sitting next to the north environment sensor.

Mission 6
  - A hole into a lava tube: 186 m south-west. A big dark pit with boulders
    around it. Press E at the orange marker on the rim. You can't fall in.
  - Who measured Moon radiation?: 140 m south-south-east. A gold detector
    box with a thin antenna.

Mission 7 (both are far away: take the rover, get out, then press E)
  - Ice you can see: 226 m north-east, inside the crater beside Science
    Site C. Walk down into the dark part. Switching on your helmet lamp (F)
    makes the ice sparkle, which makes it easier to spot.
  - The robot that waited: 235 m south-west. An old three-legged robot
    lander with an antenna mast.

Mission 8
  - Impact flash!: 141 m south. A white telescope on a tripod. In the
    cutscene a space rock hits the plain.
  - The horizon glow mystery: 148 m north-west. A small white dust detector.

Mission 9
  - The mirror on the Moon: 167 m south. A tilted panel of round mirrors on
    short legs. A green laser from Earth bounces off it.
  - When Earth hides the Sun: 151 m north-east. A camera on a tripod
    labelled ECLIPSE CAM-2. The cutscene jumps ahead to an eclipse.
